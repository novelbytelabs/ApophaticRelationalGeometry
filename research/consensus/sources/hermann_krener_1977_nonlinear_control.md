## Exact contribution used

Hermann and Krener develop nonlinear analogues of controllability, observability, and minimal realization using differential-geometric tools. Control vector fields and their Lie brackets generate accessibility distributions, while output functions and their iterated Lie derivatives generate observability codistributions. Rank conditions provide local conclusions under stated smoothness and regularity assumptions.

The load-bearing result for CS1 is that nonlinear reachability and distinguishability depend on trajectory-local geometry and on the interaction among dynamics, control directions, outputs, and state. They are not captured by one global matrix projection or one scalar alignment measure.

## Claim type

- mathematical definitions;
- local differential-geometric rank criteria;
- nonlinear realization framework.

## Assumptions and domain

The systems are smooth finite-dimensional control systems on manifolds. Conclusions distinguish local, weak, global, and strong notions; rank conditions have scoped sufficient or generic implications rather than one universal global guarantee.

The theory does not establish that Lie-generated directions are ontologically fundamental or that local accessibility implies practical controllability under constraints.

## Evidence and verification status

- **Derivation or protocol:** differential geometry, Lie algebras of vector fields, codistributions, and inverse-function arguments.

## Equation or theorem mapping

For a control-affine system,

$$
\dot x=f_0(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_i f_i(x),
\qquad
y=h(x),
$$

accessibility is analyzed using the Lie algebra generated by the vector fields $f_i$. Observability is analyzed using differentials of $h$ and its iterated Lie derivatives along the system vector fields.

A representative observability rank condition tests whether the generated codistribution has full rank at a point. The precise conclusion depends on analyticity, regularity, and the chosen observability notion.

## Cross-field implications

This literature is a direct precedent for trajectory-dependent “compatibility.” In nonlinear systems, the relevant relationship can be generated dynamically through brackets and Lie derivatives rather than pre-existing as an eigenvector overlap. It also demonstrates that controllability and observability are distinct relational questions.

## Consensus assessment

- **Proposed grade:** C4 for the existence and scoped use of nonlinear accessibility and observability geometry; C3–C4 for particular rank-condition conclusions depending on hypotheses.
- **Scoped consensus statement:** Nonlinear reachability and distinguishability depend on the local differential geometry generated jointly by system dynamics, controls, outputs, and state.
- **Reason for grade:** foundational theorem-level framework with mature uptake.
- **What remains disputed or open:** global, constrained, noisy, hybrid, and changing-dimensional generalizations; practical computation at scale.

## Novelty implication for ARG

- nonlinear state–dynamics interaction geometry is established;
- trajectory-local generated directions are not a new ARG discovery;
- a single compatibility scalar is unlikely to capture nonlinear controllability and observability generally;
- any ARG residual must survive comparison with accessibility distributions, observability codistributions, and their constrained extensions.

## Experiment decision

- **Is a new experiment needed?** no to establish nonlinear state-dependent reachability and observability.
- **Exact unresolved question:** whether ARG defines a representation-robust quantity useful beyond established differential-geometric criteria in adaptive or topology-changing systems.
- **Minimum discriminating experiment or proof:** compare predictions against rank, accessibility, observability, and reachable-set baselines on systems where those quantities are computable.
