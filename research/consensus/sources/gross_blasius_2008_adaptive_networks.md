# Consensus Source Note — Gross and Blasius on Adaptive Coevolutionary Networks

## Citation

Thilo Gross and Bernd Blasius. “Adaptive Coevolutionary Networks: A Review.” *Journal of the Royal Society Interface* 5, no. 20 (2008): 259–271. DOI: 10.1098/rsif.2007.1229. PMID: 17971320; PMCID: PMC2405905.

## Source classification

- **Field:** complex systems and biological physics

- **Primary or secondary:** authoritative field synthesis

## Exact contribution used

The review identifies adaptive networks as systems in which the dynamics on network nodes and the topology of the network coevolve. It compares work across biological, social, and game-theoretic applications and identifies common themes, including complex dynamics and robust topological self-organization generated by local rules.

For CS1, the source establishes that treating connection structure as dynamically changing alongside node states is a standard and widely studied modeling architecture. The coupling between state evolution and topology evolution is not an ARG novelty.

## Claim type

- established modeling framework;
- authoritative cross-application review;
- scientific and mathematical synthesis.

## Assumptions and domain

Adaptive-network models specify:

- node-state variables;
- network or edge variables;
- a node-state update rule;
- a topology update or rewiring rule;
- coupling between those update processes.

Specific results depend on the chosen model, time scale, stochasticity, graph class, and observables. The review does not imply that every physical or biological structure should be represented as a graph.

## Evidence and verification status

- **Derivation or protocol:** review of multiple established adaptive-network models.
- **Data or code available:** model-specific; not audited in this initial note.
- **Independent replication or reproduction:** field-level uptake is broad; detailed citation audit pending.
- **Known counterexamples or exceptions:** many systems are adequately modeled on fixed networks; coevolution is not always identifiable from observed node trajectories alone.

## Equation or theorem mapping

A generic adaptive network can be represented schematically as

$$
\dot x=F(x,B),
\qquad
\dot B=G(B,x).
$$

ARG’s coevolving state and relational variables are an instance of this broad architecture when $B$ is represented by graph, edge, or metric variables.

### Mapping conditions

The mapping requires explicit identification of ARG relational variables with network topology or weights. Calling dynamic weights “geometry” does not create a mathematical distinction unless additional metric or invariant structure is proved.

## Cross-field implications

This literature shows that “state affects structure and structure affects state” is already a central principle in adaptive-network science. It also sharpens the representation problem: variables treated as topology in one model may be dynamic state variables in an augmented system.

## Consensus assessment

- **Proposed grade:** C4 for the scoped modeling statement that node and topology dynamics can be coupled and are extensively studied.
- **Scoped consensus statement:** Adaptive-network models explicitly couple dynamics on nodes to dynamics of topology, allowing state and relational organization to coevolve.
- **Reason for grade:** established field with an authoritative review and broad application base.
- **What remains disputed or open:** system-specific mechanisms, identifiability, causal interpretation, and whether a geometric or ontological reading adds explanatory value.

## Novelty implication for ARG

- adaptive state–structure coevolution is a standard established ingredient;
- ARG must demonstrate more than coevolving node and edge variables;
- candidate residuals may involve a precise combination with constraints, invariants, transport, or a cross-field synthesis not reducible to standard adaptive-network notation.

## Experiment decision

- **Is a new experiment needed?** no to establish that states and network structure may coevolve or jointly determine dynamics.
- **Exact unresolved question:** whether an ARG-specific mechanism or invariant distinguishes its models from matched adaptive-network alternatives.
- **Minimum discriminating experiment or proof:** direct equation mapping and reparameterization audit first; experiment only if a non-equivalent residual remains.
