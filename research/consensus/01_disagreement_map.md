# CS1 Preliminary Disagreement Map

## Status

> **INITIAL — based on the first three source baskets.**

This map separates accepted mathematical structure from live questions of interpretation, modeling choice, and ontology.

---

# 1. State

## Established core

- A state is part of a dynamical representation.
- In linear realization theory, different internal realizations can produce the same input/output behavior.
- Unreachable or unobservable state variables may be redundant for a declared input/output task.

## Live disagreement or choice

- Whether state variables correspond to physically real internal properties.
- Which realization is explanatorily or ontologically privileged.
- Whether parameters, topology, environment, and rules should be included in an enlarged state.

## ARG rule

Treat the state–structure split as a model factorization until an invariant distinction is established.

---

# 2. Persistence

## Established core

Distinct mathematical predicates exist for:

- norm or energy retention;
- basin return;
- invariant-manifold continuation;
- metastable ensemble retention;
- structural stability;
- viability;
- positive invariance;
- controlled invariance.

## Live disagreement or choice

- Which predicate captures identity, function, survival, memory, or organization in a target system.
- Whether persistence is autonomous or sustained by active correction.
- Which observable, norm, constraint, equivalence relation, perturbation class, and horizon are appropriate.

## ARG rule

Do not use bare `persistence` as a primitive scientific quantity.

---

# 3. Compatibility

## Established core

Multiple scoped relational quantities already measure how state interacts with system structure:

- modal projection;
- propagator gain;
- singular-vector alignment;
- basin membership;
- controllability and observability Gramians;
- Hankel singular values;
- accessibility and observability distributions;
- viability and invariant-set membership.

## Live disagreement or choice

- Whether these quantities instantiate one useful abstract schema.
- Whether any nontrivial invariant survives across linear, nonlinear, control, biological, and philosophical contexts.
- Whether one scalar could ever preserve the distinctions.

## ARG rule

`State–structure compatibility` remains C1 umbrella language, not a discovered universal law.

---

# 4. Intervention and causation

## Established core

Control theory specifies how declared inputs can move a system and whether a policy can preserve a constraint set.

## Live disagreement or choice

- Whether controllability constitutes causal influence in a philosophical sense.
- Which interventions are physically available rather than merely mathematically admissible.
- Whether a macrovariable has autonomous causal status or only summarizes lower-level dynamics.

## ARG rule

Do not infer metaphysical causation from controllability or feedback dependence alone.

---

# 5. Observation and information

## Established core

Observability concerns distinguishability of internal states from output histories under declared dynamics and measurements.

## Live disagreement or choice

- How observability relates to Shannon information, semantic content, epistemic access, or consciousness.
- Which outputs count as physically meaningful observables.
- Whether observational equivalence implies theoretical equivalence.

## ARG rule

Do not substitute `information` for `observability` before the information-theory basket.

---

# 6. Representation equivalence

## Established core

Different criteria preserve different content:

- input/output equivalence;
- minimal realization;
- similarity transformation;
- balanced realization;
- topological conjugacy;
- definitional equivalence;
- categorical equivalence;
- interpretational equivalence.

## Live disagreement or choice

- Which criterion captures sameness for a physical theory.
- Whether empirically equivalent formulations differ in ontology or explanatory structure.
- Which coordinate, metric, probabilistic, symplectic, causal, or semantic structures must be preserved.

## ARG rule

Specify the equivalence criterion for every representation-robust claim.

---

# 7. Transport through structural change

## Established core

Smoothly evolving spaces can be related through explicit pushforward and pullback maps satisfying consistency conditions.

## Live disagreement or open problem

- Whether a transport is canonical, selected, or inferred.
- How to handle discrete resets, dimension changes, merges, splits, deletion, and creation.
- Which quantities should be conserved during remeshing.
- Whether graph matching identifies physical continuity or only optimal correspondence under a cost.

## ARG rule

No persistence-under-change claim is complete until its transport and preservation semantics are explicit.

---

# 8. Ontology

## Established core

None of the audited mathematical frameworks establishes that reality fundamentally consists of states, graphs, manifolds, constraints, controls, relations, or information.

## Live disagreement

- Structural realism versus object-based realism.
- Process ontology versus substance ontology.
- Whether relations require relata.
- Whether mathematical equivalence constrains ontology.
- Whether pluralistic descriptions imply pluralistic reality.

## ARG rule

These questions remain C2–C0 depending on formulation. They do not enter the Minimal ARG Core as scientific results.

---

# 9. Current non-disagreement

The following should no longer be treated as open ARG discoveries:

- trajectories depend on dynamics and initial state;
- different initial states can behave differently under one operator;
- state and topology can coevolve;
- nonlinear robustness depends on basin and invariant-set organization;
- reachability and observability depend on system, inputs, outputs, and state;
- viability and invariant-set preservation are established;
- transport requires explicit identification machinery;
- representation equivalence is criterion-dependent;
- persistence has multiple inequivalent formal meanings.
