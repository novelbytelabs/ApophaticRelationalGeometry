## Purpose

The workspace prevents ARG from treating a familiar result, a cross-field analogy, or an interpretive preference as a new scientific discovery.

For every topic, the workflow is:

$$
\text{atomic claims}
\to
\text{established terminology}
\to
\text{primary evidence}
\to
\text{cross-field translation}
\to
\text{consensus grade}
\to
\text{residual question}.
$$

Experiments are proposed only for residual questions that remain unresolved after this process.

## Source standards

Priority order:

1. original paper containing the theorem, experiment, or model;
2. authoritative monograph or review establishing field consensus;
3. official code or data linked by the authors;
4. independent reproduction or adversarial critique;
5. secondary source for orientation only.

A technical claim may not rest solely on a search snippet, abstract paraphrase, promotional page, social post, or an uncited model summary.

## Required source-note content

Every source note must record:

- the exact claim, theorem, equation, experiment, or observation used;
- the assumptions and scope;
- whether the result is mathematical, empirical, mechanistic, interpretive, or metaphysical;
- the established terminology;
- the mapping into ARG vocabulary;
- whether the mapping is identity, translation, specialization, approximation, analogy, or loose resemblance;
- consensus and novelty implications;
- known exceptions and live disagreements;
- whether an experiment is still needed.

## Working discipline

- Do not infer novelty from unfamiliar terminology.
- Do not infer ontology from mathematical usefulness.
- Do not merge different meanings of `state`, `structure`, `relation`, `constraint`, `information`, or `persistence`.
- Do not reproduce an established result unless reproduction is required to validate code or test a precise extension.
- Preserve negative findings, no-novelty conclusions, failed translations, and unresolved disagreements.
- Update the project claim ledger and novelty matrix only after the supporting source notes exist.

## Acceptance rule

A statement may enter the Minimal ARG Core only when it is:

- graded C3 or C4;
- scoped to the domain actually supported;
- robust under the relevant admissible representations;
- stated without importing a disputed interpretation;
- traceable to completed source notes.
