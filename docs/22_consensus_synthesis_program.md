# ARG Consensus Synthesis Program

## Status

> **Candidate convergence synthesis — not a claim of a new fundamental geometry, a completed ontology, or a novel scientific law.**

This program changes the order of operations for Apophatic Relational Geometry (ARG):

$$
\boxed{
\text{research first}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{synthesize second}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{formalize third}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{experiment only on the unresolved remainder}.
}
$$

The purpose is to determine what picture of relation, change, persistence, causation, organization, scale, and representation is already constrained by established mathematics, reproducible science, and disciplined philosophy. ARG may contribute a useful synthesis even when every ingredient is already known. Synthesis is not itself evidence of novelty or truth.

This track is parallel to the existing four-model software and experiment track. It does **not** authorize the blocked development pilot, change a frozen protocol, or promote any model claim.

---

# 1. Governing question

ARG asks:

> **What is the strongest minimal account of structured becoming that survives translation across mathematics, physics, dynamical systems, computer science, biology, and philosophy without exceeding the evidence of any field?**

The goal is not to make every discipline use one vocabulary. Different perspectives may describe different aspects, scales, or inferential roles of the same phenomenon. The research task is to identify:

1. where the fields genuinely agree;
2. where statements are exact translations, specializations, or analogies;
3. where accepted observations coexist with interpretive disagreement;
4. which proposed ARG statements are already established;
5. which statements remain open;
6. whether any precise residual contribution remains after the audit.

---

# 2. What cross-disciplinary consensus means

Consensus is not a vote among disciplines. It is convergence under independent constraints.

A candidate consensus statement must satisfy all applicable tests:

- **mathematical coherence** — definitions are typed, assumptions explicit, and deductions valid;
- **empirical adequacy** — where the claim is empirical, it is supported by reproducible observations or experiments;
- **mechanistic adequacy** — where a mechanism is claimed, interventions or difference-making evidence distinguish it from alternatives;
- **representational robustness** — the claim survives admissible relabelings, coordinate changes, or equivalent reformulations;
- **scope discipline** — conclusions remain within the domain established by the evidence;
- **cross-field translatability** — mappings state what is preserved and where the mapping fails;
- **apophatic restraint** — no successful model term is promoted into an absolute ground.

A field contributes according to its competence:

| Perspective | Primary contribution | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | Possibility, consistency, invariance, equivalence, theorem and counterexample | Which formal structure is physically realized |
| Physics | Quantitative constraints from observation, experiment, symmetry, and successful theory | A unique metaphysical interpretation of all successful formalisms |
| Dynamical systems and control | Stability, attractors, transients, state dependence, intervention, controllability | Ontological fundamentality |
| Computer science and information theory | Representation, computation, state transition, encoding, complexity, recoverability | That reality literally is a computation or data structure |
| Biology and systems science | Organization, function, adaptation, development, multiscale persistence | Fundamental physical ontology |
| Philosophy | Conceptual clarification, inference limits, ontology, explanation, and category-error detection | Replacement of mathematical proof or empirical evidence |

# 3. Evidence and consensus grades

Every substantive statement must be classified by both **claim type** and **consensus grade**.

## Claim types

- mathematical definition;
- mathematical theorem;
- empirical regularity;
- mechanistic claim;
- modeling convention;
- interpretive thesis;
- metaphysical conjecture.

## Consensus grades

- **C4 — Established:** textbook-level theorem, repeatedly confirmed empirical result, or mature field consensus within explicit scope.
- **C3 — Strong convergence:** multiple independent lines of evidence or methods agree, with bounded exceptions.
- **C2 — Accepted core, disputed interpretation:** the result is accepted but its meaning, explanation, or ontology remains contested.
- **C1 — Plausible synthesis:** compatible with established results but not independently established.
- **C0 — Open or speculative:** conjecture, unresolved question, or proposed research direction.
- **R — Rejected:** contradicted, disproved, withdrawn, or superseded within the declared scope.

Only C3 and C4 statements may enter the Minimal ARG Core. C2 statements belong in the disagreement map. C0 and C1 statements belong in the residual research agenda.

Each consensus slice must produce:

2. **Source notes** — exact theorem, experiment, equation, assumptions, and limitations.
3. **Consensus table** — claim, type, grade, scope, sources, exceptions, and ARG consequence.

5. **Disagreement map** — accepted core separated from competing interpretations.

9. **Formalism impact note** — what must change in ARG notation and claims.
10. **Claim-ledger amendments** — promotion, narrowing, rejection, or quarantine.

# 6. Translation discipline

A proposed cross-field bridge must be labeled as one of:

- identity;
- exact translation;
- specialization;
- generalization;
- approximation;
- analogy;
- loose resemblance.

Every bridge must state:

1. source and destination objects;
2. assumptions;
3. preserved properties;
4. lost properties;
5. failure cases;
6. whether the bridge supports prediction, explanation, or interpretation.

Analogies may guide research but do not establish identity or mechanism. Loose resemblance does not support an ARG proposition.

# 7. Typed scientific-context rule

The completed control-theory basket demonstrates that a bare structure–state pair is often insufficient for a scientific claim.

For an autonomous trajectory question, a schematic model may use

$$
Q_t=(B_t,x_t),
\qquad
x_{t+1}=F_{B_t}(x_t).
$$

But intervention, observation, viability, invariance, robustness, or recoverability claims may require a typed context such as

$$
\mathcal S=
(\mathcal X,F,B_{\mathrm{in}},C_{\mathrm{out}},K,\mathcal U,\mathcal W,\Phi,T),
$$

where:

- $\mathcal X$ is the state space;
- $F$ is the transition law or admissible transition family;
- $B_{\mathrm{in}}$ is an input or intervention map;
- $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ is an output or observation map;
- $K$ is a constraint or admissibility set;
- $\mathcal U$ is the admissible control class;
- $\mathcal W$ is a disturbance or uncertainty class;
- $\Phi$ is the measured functional;
- $T$ is the horizon.

The tuple is a methodological typing requirement, not a final ontology. No component may be omitted when the claim depends on it.

# 8. Research gates

## Gate A — scope

- phenomenon and atomic claims fixed;
- claim types identified;
- prohibited inferences listed;
- primary fields and search vocabulary declared.

## Gate B — source sufficiency

- load-bearing claims traced to primary sources;
- authoritative reviews used to establish field structure;
- counterexamples and scope limits recorded;
- no technical claim rests solely on secondary summaries.

## Gate C — translation

- mappings classified;
- assumptions and losses explicit;
- terminology collisions resolved;
- perspective-specific information preserved.

## Gate D — consensus

- grades assigned claim by claim;
- C2 disagreements preserved;
- C0–C1 proposals excluded from the Minimal Core;
- independent reviewer can trace every row to its evidence.

## Gate E — residual

A new proof or experiment is authorized only if:

- the exact residual is not already established;
- an established method cannot answer it without modification;
- the test discriminates among live alternatives;
- the result would change a claim, model, or research decision;
- controls, nulls, and stop conditions are fixed first.

---

# 9. Stop rules

Stop a novelty claim, formal extension, or experiment when:

- a standard theorem already establishes the result;
- ARG merely renames a known quantity;
- a proposed experiment reproduces a known qualitative effect;
- the claim changes under an admissible representation and no invariant content is identified;
- the evidence supports several interpretations but no discriminating test exists;
- a bridge is analogy rather than translation;
- a proposed ontology exceeds the weakest supporting discipline.

In those cases, cite, synthesize, and narrow.
